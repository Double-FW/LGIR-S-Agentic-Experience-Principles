# Satisfactory experience, measurement and research

Informative · LGIRS-5.1–5.5

Satisfactory is an experience principle. Satisfaction is a person's assessment, gathered with a stated method and context. They are related but not interchangeable. A person can like an answer that is wrong or accept a burdensome service because better access has never been available.

## Define success around the actual purpose

State the person's goal, material constraints and what would make the result correct and useful. A booking ID, generated report or resolved ticket is an output. Establish whether the journey is usable, the report supports its decision or the issue was actually resolved. Include adverse effects and unmet constraints rather than selecting only the positive endpoint.

Preserve dignity, identity, choice and control as substantive outcomes. Some failures require action even when the group is small or an aggregate score improves. A favourable rating cannot waive an applicable requirement, correct a false conclusion or authorise a prohibited action.

## Measure total human burden

Capture relevant work across briefing, providing context, waiting, supervision, verification, correction, support and recovery. Separate active time from elapsed time and define how concurrent activity is counted. Do not add overlapping categories twice. Distinguish chosen review from forced checking caused by unreliable output, while retaining both where they consume real effort.

For a defined comparison:

`difference in active human time = comparable baseline active time - agent-route active time`

Use the same task-quality threshold and appropriately supported access conditions. Report distributions, exclusions and uncertainty, not only a mean. A positive difference is not proof of benefit if correctness, dignity or access deteriorates. Agent generation latency is a different metric and must not be presented as total human time saved.

## Suggested evidence plan

| Outcome | Evidence | Important qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Correct/useful completion | Task-specific inspection and observed outcomes | Define the goal independently of the tool's success flag |
| Time/effort | Active/elapsed time, corrections, assistance and recovery | Match scopes and avoid double counting |
| Navigation/orientation | Observed choices, resumption and questions | Faster movement is not always better understanding |
| Content use | Comprehension and application to the intended decision | Dwell time or scroll depth is not comprehension |
| Preferences/choice | Actual availability and equivalent task outcomes | A settings control does not prove preferences persist |
| Dignity/control | Voluntary accounts and observed service differences | Do not assign feelings or infer identity from behaviour |
| Satisfaction | Clear questions, reasons and context | Report whose experience was actually captured |

## Include the relevant people

Recruit for actual goals, functional needs, access modes, familiarity and circumstances. Include disability-related, cognitive, fluctuating and intersecting needs relevant to the service, without assuming a diagnosis dictates capability. Include non-completers and people excluded from recruitment or the current channel where possible; document missing coverage.

Explain purpose, data use, recording, voluntary participation and withdrawal. Make consent and activities accessible, permit breaks and appropriate support, and collect the minimum necessary data. Service use is not automatic research consent. Do not publish identifiable participant, employee or complaint records in this repository or as training examples. Off-estate collection alone does not establish legal compliance; review actual processing and responsibilities.

## Example short questions

- Did you achieve what you came to do? What remains unresolved?
- What did you need to check or correct yourself?
- Did you understand what the agent had and had not done?
- Could you change your mind, pace or support route when you needed to?
- Did the experience treat you with respect? What made you say that?

These are research prompts, not a validated scale. Avoid requiring every person to answer every question after every low-impact action. A brief rating can locate issues; follow-up context helps interpret them. Do not claim population satisfaction from a self-selected sample or statistical significance without a suitable study and analysis.

## Compare LGIR+S carefully

To investigate added value over an existing process, define the baseline precisely: a narrow interface checklist, a full accessibility/UX practice or another agentic method are different comparators. Preserve accessibility in all conditions. Use ethically appropriate pilots, account for learning/order effects and differences in task difficulty, and report failures, non-response and uncertainty.

The eight examples in this repository are hypotheses about better-targeted questions. They contain no measured time savings, completed participant study or proof of superiority. Route findings to a named improvement owner, retest changes and keep complaint/incident evidence alongside research without treating complaints as a complete satisfaction measure.
