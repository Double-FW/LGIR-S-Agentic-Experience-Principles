# Research agenda and open questions

LGIR+S is an editorial proposal. Its completeness, criterion reliability, tier assignments and practical value have not been independently validated. A polished document structure is not evidence that a framework works.

## Questions to investigate

1. Do reviewers interpret each criterion consistently for the same declared scope? Compare independent assessments and reasons, not just agreement on an overall score.
2. Does the extra agentic framing identify relevant issues beyond an organisation's existing accessibility, UX, security and AI-governance practice? Name the baseline accurately; a narrow checklist is not all of POUR.
3. Can users understand and use the proposed task/status/authority representations across relevant access needs and contexts?
4. Which forms of progress, explanation and uncertainty help decisions without excessive interruption or misplaced confidence?
5. When do controls produce effective intervention, and when does system speed, latency or complexity make oversight nominal?
6. Does total human burden improve for the intended task and people, including checking, correction and unsuccessful attempts?
7. Are local A/AA labels useful, or would named priority categories avoid unhelpful comparisons with WCAG? No validated level structure currently exists.
8. Which requirements need splitting to become reliably testable? Combined concepts such as appropriate effort or effective explanation need task-specific operational definitions.
9. How should evidence be maintained when supplier training lineage, transaction visibility or rollback capabilities are incomplete?
10. What changes are needed for voice-only services, embodied systems, safety-critical services or consequential background decisions? These need specialist domain methods, not an unmodified web test.

## Proposed staged research

Start with expert review of scope, terminology and criterion clarity, including disability/accessibility, engineering, security, research and relevant governance expertise. Use fictional desk cases to find ambiguities, while stating that desk review is not real-world validation.

Next, undertake a bounded prototype pilot with defined tasks, appropriate accessible recruitment and consent, predeclared outcomes and an ethical comparison. Keep applicable accessibility and safety requirements in every condition. Account for task differences, familiarity, learning/order effects and model variability.

Report adverse outcomes, exclusion, non-response and participant burden. Include people who could not enter or complete the main route where practicable. Do not generalise a small self-selected study to all disabled people or every model. Publish aggregate findings and method limitations without personal operational records.

Then examine repeated use and changes over time: whether trust stays calibrated, people retain control, corrections feed back into the service and model/tool updates cause regressions. Material changes should lead to re-evaluation, not an assertion of evergreen robustness.

## Claims not yet supported

- LGIR+S is superior to POUR, WCAG or another comprehensive method.
- The 25 criteria are exhaustive, independently reproducible or a validated safety/conformance scheme.
- A/AA tiers establish a legal standard, external approval or an overall quality level.
- The examples demonstrate measured time savings, actual participant experience or production reliability.
- A schema, metadata field or guardrail pattern prevents every harmful action, hallucination or prompt injection.

These limits do not prevent a transparent local pilot. They define what evidence is needed before stronger claims.
