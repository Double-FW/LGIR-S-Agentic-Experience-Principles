# Adopt LGIR+S in a bounded project

This is a practical starting method. Suggested roles and outputs require actual local ownership and capacity; they do not appoint anyone or approve a deployment.

## 1. Choose one useful task

Select a defined agent-assisted task and the decision the pilot will support. Identify intended benefits and foreseeable harms, including people affected without directly using the interface. Record the complete task, constraints, existing accessible alternatives and what would count as correct/useful completion.

Output: a completed [project brief](../templates/project-brief.md), with unknowns retained.

## 2. Establish authority and boundaries

Name the actual task owner, technical/security/data specialists as relevant, research owner and release/operational decision-maker. Identify who can delegate, approve, intervene and challenge. Record effective access, data restrictions, budgets, irreversible effects and required external requirements. Legal applicability requires actual source and specialist review, not inference from an acronym.

Output: a [delegation record](../templates/delegation-record.md) and source/authority gaps.

## 3. Select and interpret the criteria

Read the five principles and select relevant criteria for the scope. Use their Understanding pages, not a title-only checklist. Record applicability reasons and remaining coverage. Do not turn provisional tiers into a score or exclude a serious issue merely because it is labelled AA.

Output: an initial [assessment record](../templates/assessment-record.md), all selected results initially Not evaluated.

## 4. Design the system and human experience together

Connect source provenance and trust boundaries, task/permission contracts, actual execution events, recovery and accessible status/intervention. Define how handovers and model changes will be managed. Use existing design and branding systems where appropriate; verify configured components and complete journeys.

Output: design decisions, owners, versioned contracts and planned evidence. A diagram is not an implemented integration.

## 5. Exercise failures before real commitment

Run controlled ambiguity, attack, timeout, partial-effect, concurrency, revocation and recovery scenarios relevant to the task. Inspect actual attempted effects and event records. Test relevant access modes. Record failures, unsupported combinations and unknown outcomes rather than inventing a completed recovery.

Output: actual test records with scope and limitations. The written probes in this repository remain unexecuted until the project runs them.

## 6. Investigate the person's experience

Use accessible, proportionate research with relevant people and appropriate privacy arrangements. Include total human effort, useful outcomes, control, support choices, dignity and reasons for satisfaction or dissatisfaction. Include people excluded from the successful path, and report missing coverage. Use [the experience study template](../templates/experience-study.md).

Output: observations and participant accounts kept separate from estimates, explanations and hypotheses.

## 7. Make and record the actual decision

Review serious failures, applicable obligations, benefits, burdens and unresolved gaps together. Implement and retest corrections where needed. The authorised person makes the scoped release/pilot decision, with constraints and review triggers. Do not automate acceptance of risk through a generated signature or average score.

Output: a real [decision and change record](../templates/decision-and-change-record.md).

## 8. Monitor changes and improve

Review incidents, complaints, abandonment, corrections and experience evidence. Re-evaluate material model, prompt, retrieval, tool, policy or oversight changes before affected release. Record versions and outcomes. Monitoring cadence depends on the service; no recurring job is installed by this runbook.
