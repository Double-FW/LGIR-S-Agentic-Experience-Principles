# Understanding LGIRS-5.4: Dignity and comparable opportunity

Informative · Editor’s Draft 0.1.0 · No product result established

[Specification](../SPECIFICATION.md#lgirs-5-4) · [Understanding index](README.md)

## Candidate criterion

Evaluation MUST examine material differences in dignity, privacy, control, service quality and opportunity across relevant access routes and affected groups; favourable averages MUST NOT erase a serious excluded outcome.

**Local priority:** A (provisional; not WCAG conformance).

## Intent and potential benefit

The same endpoint can conceal unequal treatment and avoidable dependence.

This is a rationale to investigate, not a measured effectiveness claim. Confirm its relevance with the affected people and the actual service.

## Applicability

Customers, employees and other affected people across relevant needs and contexts, including people unable to start or finish.

## Implementation approach

Combine task evidence with voluntary accounts and exposure-aware analysis; keep group definitions and sample limits explicit without inferring diagnoses.

See [technique T24](../implementation/techniques.md#t24). The approach is informative; document and evaluate an alternative if it achieves the scoped outcome.

## Evaluation probe — not yet run

1. Identify the task, roles, authorised scope, product/model/tool versions and expected evidence. Agree any service-specific timing and safety limits before testing.
2. Prepare controlled, synthetic or appropriately authorised test conditions; do not cause real irreversible effects merely to test a failure route.
3. Compare a private self-service route with an assisted route requiring public disclosure, extra cost or reduced availability.
4. Observe: The differences are recorded as material outcomes and investigated even if overall satisfaction is high.
5. Capture actual events, accessible presentation and human observations separately. Record missing coverage and contrary evidence. Assign the criterion result using the evaluation method; do not infer effectiveness from this written probe.

## Failure example

A positive average rating hides that the assisted route requires humiliating disclosure.

## Evidence and limits

Retain the requirement ID, exact evaluated scope, method, observed outcome, source/version context, reviewer and unresolved gaps. A generated expected result is not an observation. Machine checks, human inspection, specialist evaluation and participant research have different roles. Passing this probe alone does not establish conformance with WCAG or reliability for all contexts.
