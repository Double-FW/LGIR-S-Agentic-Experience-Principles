# Understanding LGIRS-5.3: Choice, preferences and assistance

Informative · Editor’s Draft 0.1.0 · No product result established

[Specification](../SPECIFICATION.md#lgirs-5-3) · [Understanding index](README.md)

## Candidate criterion

The experience MUST support relevant choices of pace, presentation, assistance and delegation within real service constraints, with workable alternatives and explicit limits.

**Local priority:** A (provisional; not WCAG conformance).

## Intent and potential benefit

A person's preferred level of autonomy or support can change during the task.

This is a rationale to investigate, not a measured effectiveness claim. Confirm its relevance with the affected people and the actual service.

## Applicability

Human interaction and service channels; alternatives must be evaluated for material burdens and outcomes, not merely listed.

## Implementation approach

Offer adjustable notification/detail, save-and-return and supported human routes; avoid diagnosis-based defaults and coercive agent-only channels.

See [technique T23](../implementation/techniques.md#t23). The approach is informative; document and evaluate an alternative if it achieves the scoped outcome.

## Evaluation probe — not yet run

1. Identify the task, roles, authorised scope, product/model/tool versions and expected evidence. Agree any service-specific timing and safety limits before testing.
2. Prepare controlled, synthetic or appropriately authorised test conditions; do not cause real irreversible effects merely to test a failure route.
3. A person changes from autonomous drafting to assisted review and later to a human channel while using a non-visual interface.
4. Observe: The chosen route preserves appropriate context, privacy and intended service quality without avoidable repeated disclosure or loss of valid work.
5. Capture actual events, accessible presentation and human observations separately. Record missing coverage and contrary evidence. Assign the criterion result using the evaluation method; do not infer effectiveness from this written probe.

## Failure example

The only accessible alternative is a slower paid channel available for fewer hours.

## Evidence and limits

Retain the requirement ID, exact evaluated scope, method, observed outcome, source/version context, reviewer and unresolved gaps. A generated expected result is not an observation. Machine checks, human inspection, specialist evaluation and participant research have different roles.
