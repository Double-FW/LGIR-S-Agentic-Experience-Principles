# EX03: A research assistant creates a fast but expensive-to-check report

Fictional illustrative case · No product trial performed

[Examples index](README.md) · [Summary mapping](../docs/web-wcag-vs-agentic-ai.md)

## Situation

An analyst requests a sourced briefing. The assistant generates it quickly, but some citations are outdated, one source is restricted and several statements need extensive verification.

## What a narrow interface-focused POUR review might examine

A review may examine headings, readable text, meaningful links, keyboard navigation and predictable document structure.

These observations would not establish a full WCAG result; actual applicable criteria and complete processes still need evaluation.

## Agentic issue that remains

The report can be accessible while its facts, provenance and total human checking burden remain unacceptable for the task.

## Applying LGIR+S

- **Legible:** Distinguish retrieved facts, synthesis, estimates and evidence gaps with accessible provenance detail.
- **Governable:** Keep publication outside the existing drafting authority and offer a reviewable correction route.
- **Interpretable:** Explain conflicting sources and relevant dates; do not present self-reported confidence as a measured probability.
- **Robust:** Carry source identity, dates, transformations and qualifications through retrieval and export without inventing missing lineage.
- **Satisfactory:** Count the analyst’s source checking, correction and recovery work in the comparison, alongside correctness and usefulness.

## Why this adds a better-targeted agentic question

LGIR+S makes accountable evidence handling and the human work transferred by automation explicit. A shorter generation time is insufficient to claim a better experience.

The proposed advantage is specificity for this agentic issue, not demonstrated general superiority or a reason to remove accessibility requirements.

## Evaluate the proposal

- Include known conflicting versions, a missing source and a restricted source in a controlled brief.
- Compare actual correct task completion and total review effort against an appropriate accessible alternative.
- Ask participants about usefulness and burden while preserving source errors as separate findings.

## Limits

This is a proposed research design, not measured time saving. Do not expose protected sources in a public briefing or infer legal currency from a new access date.
