# EX06: A travel agent optimises price but misses the actual goal

Fictional illustrative case · No product trial performed

[Examples index](README.md) · [Summary mapping](../docs/web-wcag-vs-agentic-ai.md)

## Situation

A traveller requests a cheaper journey with an accessible transfer, manageable connection time and a preferred assistance arrangement. The agent returns a cheaper confirmed booking that violates a material requirement.

## What a narrow interface-focused POUR review might examine

A review may assess the accessible search, selection, booking and confirmation interfaces, including errors and complete processes.

These observations would not establish a full WCAG result; actual applicable criteria and complete processes still need evaluation.

## Agentic issue that remains

The agent’s optimisation target and interpretation of intent need evaluation in addition to the interface. A booking ID is not sufficient evidence of the traveller’s intended outcome.

## Applying LGIR+S

- **Legible:** Keep the operative goal and material constraints available throughout replanning.
- **Governable:** Do not silently trade away a stated access requirement; obtain the necessary decision for a material change.
- **Interpretable:** Explain which constraints are verified, assumed or unavailable and the consequences of each option.
- **Robust:** Handle unavailable or changing supplier evidence and preserve the chosen constraints across integrations.
- **Satisfactory:** Judge success against the complete travel purpose, appropriate time/effort and the traveller’s account of whether the option is usable.

## Why this adds a better-targeted agentic question

LGIR+S directly asks whether an agent’s plan and outcome preserve the person’s goal. This adds specificity to agent optimisation; it is not evidence that POUR prevents task-oriented evaluation.

The proposed advantage is specificity for this agentic issue, not demonstrated general superiority or a reason to remove accessibility requirements.

## Evaluate the proposal

- Create test options where the lowest price conflicts with a required transfer feature.
- Check actual constraint retention through replanning and final booking records.
- Evaluate complete journey feasibility and the person’s verification burden without inventing supplier guarantees.

The case is fictional. Actual access arrangements and supplier commitments require current evidence; a model-generated itinerary cannot certify them.
